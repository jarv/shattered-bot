# DM-100: immediate door escape

Run 10 first saw an awake DM-100 with an observed door at cell 901, but exchanged
attacks and later used uncoordinated retreats. At the end, natural-healing waits
followed loss of visual contact; the public death message identifies lightning.

The priority tree now puts DM-100 door flight ahead of optional preparation,
loot, ranged exchanges, and ordinary recovery. Boss telegraphs, environmental
hazards, and critical emergency transport/healing retain priority.

Choose the nearest admitted **currently visible** ordinary door by observed
route length. If none is visible, a previously observed reachable door is a
fallback, not an unseen generated destination. Commit to that door while
rechecking terrain, hazards, other enemies, and movement impairment.

A door is still a useful destination when its far-side hiding bank has not yet
been seen. Approach/open it using known cells, reobserve, and only then choose
an observed safe far-side bank. Never click a guessed square beyond the door.
Step off the threshold to break sight; do not reverse toward the caster merely
because damage arrived during the final exposed transit.

Retain the commitment after the DM-100 leaves view. Remember recent public
lightning exposure for a bounded 12 turns/observations so disappearance does not
immediately authorize natural healing. A recent position constrains safety;
it is not a legal blind attack target or proof of current enemy location.

Wait off the doorway only with observed cover. Stop waiting if it opens into a
bolt line, damage occurs after an established wait, or the wait budget expires.
Use at most six waits per bank and twelve per floor. On observed adjacent entry,
attack rather than continuing to wait; a fresh door departure supports the
doorway surprise attempt. Existing robust wall-backed bank plans remain usable.

The until-death continuation path attempts the same committed door escape
before arbitrary retreat or noncritical healing. Escape routes and targets are
revalidated, not survival guarantees; unknown terrain, chasms, or a closed door
that must be crossed are not treated as permanent bolt-proof cover.
